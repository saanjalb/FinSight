## The rule
The 50/30/20 rule splits your after-tax income into three parts: 50% for needs, 30% for wants, and 20% for savings and debt repayment. It was popularised by Elizabeth Warren and Amelia Warren Tyagi.

## Needs, wants, and savings
Needs are essentials you cannot easily skip, such as rent, groceries, utilities, transport to work, and minimum loan payments. Wants are optional, such as dining out, shopping, and entertainment. Savings covers emergency funds, investments, and extra loan repayment.

## Savings rate
Your savings rate is the share of income you keep after expenses: (income − expenses) ÷ income. Under this rule the target is at least 20%.

## Adapting it
The percentages are a starting point, not a law. In an expensive city, needs may take more than 50%, so you might use 60/20/20. The value of the rule is having a simple benchmark to compare your actual spending against.
