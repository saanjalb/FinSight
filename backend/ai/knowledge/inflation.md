## What it is
Inflation is the general rise in prices over time. As prices rise, the same amount of money buys less, which is called a fall in purchasing power.

## How it is measured
In India, inflation is commonly tracked using the Consumer Price Index (CPI), which follows the cost of a typical basket of goods and services such as food, housing, and fuel.

## Example
With 6% yearly inflation, something that costs ₹100 today costs about ₹106 next year. ₹1,00,000 kept for 10 years at 6% inflation will buy only about as much as ₹55,800 buys today.

## Real returns
Your real return is roughly what your money earns minus inflation. If a deposit earns 7% while inflation is 6%, your purchasing power grows only by about 1% a year. This is why savings that earn less than inflation lose value in real terms.
