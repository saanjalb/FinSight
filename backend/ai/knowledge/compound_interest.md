## What it is
Compound interest is interest earned on both your original money (the principal) and on the interest already added. Simple interest, by contrast, is calculated only on the original principal.

## The formula
The future value is A = P × (1 + r/n)^(n×t), where P is the principal, r is the annual interest rate as a decimal, n is how many times per year interest is added, and t is the number of years.

## Example
₹10,000 at 8% a year for 10 years, compounded yearly, grows to about ₹21,589. With simple interest the same money would reach only ₹18,000. The extra ₹3,589 is interest earned on interest.

## Rule of 72
Divide 72 by the annual rate to estimate how many years it takes money to double. At 8% it takes roughly 72 ÷ 8 = 9 years. Starting early matters because the growth accelerates over time.
